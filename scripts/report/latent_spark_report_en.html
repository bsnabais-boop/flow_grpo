Steering the Latent Spark

Process rewards and GRPO for SD 1.5: what was tried, how, and what came out. Every table, chart and image comes from the result files. 6–12 November 2026.

At a glance

  • Text rendering: one run passed the independent check: the weight curriculum (alignment first, text later) at epoch 100, OCR 0.116 vs 0.103 for the base model (+0.0135, 95% interval +0.0007 to +0.0269). Real but small: 20 sampling steps give +0.049.
  • Why OCR barely moves (point 9): the reward signal is strong, but each GRPO update is almost pure noise (~1–2 × 10⁻⁴ signal per image, so a whole run adds up to only ~2× the noise), and the last denoising step took most of the update and of the KL penalty. Sharing the start noise or training only the text-forming steps does not raise the signal. Next: a differentiable text reward.
  • NoisyCLIP from Hugging Face on SD 1.5 (point 5): it was trained on SDXL latents; on SD 1.5 it only works if the latent is first turned into colours with our SD 1.5 colour map Φ, and then it is about as good as plain CLIP on that colour image.
  • New run (point 10): NoisyCLIP + OCR weight starting at 40 (not 0) + three fixes from the diagnostic. Confirmed with new seeds: alignment and image quality improve (alignment CLIP and PickScore +0.0015, text-prompt CLIP +0.007, above base 20 steps); OCR does not (−0.007, n.s.).

1Switch from SD 3.5 to SD 1.5

As asked, training moved to Stable Diffusion 1.5. The NoisyCLIP colour projection is made for 4-channel latents (SD 1.5 / SDXL); SD 3.5 has 16. SD 1.5 predicts noise (it is not flow matching), so the GRPO sampler was rewritten: a noisy DDIM step with an exact probability per step, a rank-32 LoRA, and 10 sampling steps in training.

2GRPO for text: OCR + CLIP reward

Reward = 80 × OCR + CLIPScore. OCR (PaddleOCR) is 1 when the text is exactly right and 0 when it is unrelated. CLIP changes only by thousandths within a group, so in practice OCR decides. The base is the original SD 1.5, untrained, with the same steps and seeds.

  • The model barely moved: each update changed the policy by ~1e-7 (KL).
  • Evaluating carelessly misleads: two "gains" seen during training (z = 2.5 and 1.8) disappeared in the independent check.
  • Why the reward did not turn into better text is analysed in point 9.

3Where the image and the text form

At every step the model estimates the noise; removing it gives a prediction of the final image (x̂₀). I decoded that prediction with the VAE into a preview and scored it with the training reward. 128 prompts × 8 images.

Each VAE preview + OCR costs ~1.4 UNet steps: that is the cost Phase 1 wants to remove.

4How much is lost with fewer steps

Purpose: measure how much the untrained model loses with fewer steps, before any training. It is the starting point and the yardstick for the 40–50 → 10–15 steps goal. Base SD 1.5, 1024 images per row, H200.

Takeaway: with fewer steps SD 1.5 keeps almost all of its alignment but loses a lot of text. That gap is what training has to close.

5Phase 1: NoisyCLIP as the mid-generation alignment score

Goal: know halfway through generation whether the image will match the prompt, without the VAE. As advised, I use the published NoisyCLIP (Hugging Face asiimo/noisyclip): CLIP ViT-L/14 whose image side was fine-tuned on SDXL noisy latents, turned into a small colour picture with a fixed 4→3 colour map. Its text side is the normal CLIP. SD 1.5 latents use a different colour code, so I tried three inputs: (1) the noisy latent z_t with NoisyCLIP's colour map, as in the paper; (2) the model's prediction of the final latent x̂₀ with the same map; (3) x̂₀ with Φ, a colour map fitted to SD 1.5 (15 numbers, least squares against decoded images). 128 unseen GenAI-Bench prompts × 4 images, 10 noisy steps (the GRPO setting).

Takeaway: NoisyCLIP is cheap and spots clearly wrong prompts early, but on SD 1.5 it does not transfer as is: it was trained on SDXL latents. Fed our SD 1.5 colour image it behaves like plain CLIP on that image, and it ranks images of the same prompt poorly (~0.3). Retraining it on SD 1.5 latents, or switching to SDXL, would remove the mismatch.

6Phase 2: stop bad trajectories early

GRPO generates several images per prompt. If halfway through the score already says an image will end up clearly behind the group's best, stop it and save the remaining steps. The threshold is set with Conformal Risk Control so that the chance of throwing away the group's best image stays ≤ α. Redone offline with NoisyCLIP (groups of 4, 128 prompts, calibrated on half and tested on the other half, 40 times).

Takeaway: the pruning machinery works (the risk guarantee holds), but it needs a score that ranks images of the same prompt well by mid-generation. NoisyCLIP on SD 1.5 does not (point 5), so pruning with it is no better than random.

7Phase 3: GRPO with a process reward vs an outcome reward

Focus on alignment without losing text: 1440 GenAI-Bench prompts + 1088 text prompts. Reward = 100 × alignment + 80 × OCR (OCR only on prompts with quoted text). Two runs identical except for the alignment score: PRM (process reward: alignment read mid-generation at steps 4, 6, 8, 10 with the earlier in-house scorer, no VAE) and ORM (outcome reward: CLIP on the final image). 10 steps, 32 prompts × 8 images per epoch, 100 epochs (~4.5 h each).

Takeaway: training works and the policy moves (KL ~2e-4), but neither run gave a confirmed gain. Alignment did not get worse.

8The curricula suggested by the supervisor

(1) Change the weights of alignment and OCR during training. (2) Increase the number of sampling steps during training. Same set-up as the PRM run except for the curriculum, one run at a time. Steps start at 4, not 2: with 2 steps SD 1.5 produces nothing usable. Following the supervisor's advice, the new run (point 10) starts the OCR weight at 40 instead of 0.

Takeaway: the weight curriculum is the most promising result so far and the only one confirmed: +0.013 OCR with alignment unchanged.

9Why OCR barely improves: a look inside the updates

The supervisor asked to check the hyperparameters, how strong the signal is, the spread within groups and the gradients. A diagnostic script reproduces training exactly (same sampler, reward and advantages; 32 text + 16 alignment prompts × 8 images) and computes the gradient of every single image, step by step, for the base model and two trained models.

  • Not the problem: gradient clipping never triggers (gradient norm ~0.002–0.004 vs the limit 1.0); the OCR and alignment parts of the gradient do not fight each other (unrelated directions), although on a mixed batch about half of each update goes to alignment.
  • What that means in numbers: one update would need about 7,000 images for the signal to equal the noise (we use 256). A whole 100-epoch run (25,600 images) adds up to a total of only ~1.9× the noise, which explains OCR gains of +0.01–0.02 that look significant in one evaluation and vanish with new seeds.
  • Consequence: RL on the OCR score cannot be fixed with hyperparameters. It needs ~10× more images per run (30–40 GPU-hours) or a gradient with less noise. We are trying the second first: back-propagate a differentiable text-recognition score through the last denoising steps (as in DRaFT / ReFL), keeping PaddleOCR as the independent check. If that fails, the long RL run is the fallback.

Takeaway: the reward knows which images are better, but each image carries very little usable gradient signal (and with all steps trained most of it went to the last step). Different group sampling does not change that; only many more images or a lower-noise gradient can.

10New run: NoisyCLIP + the fixes from the diagnostic

One run that follows all three pieces of advice and the diagnostic. It changes several things at once compared with the weight curriculum, so a gain would not say which change caused it.

Takeaway: first run where alignment and image quality improve steadily: CLIP and PickScore rise from ~epoch 40 to the end on both prompt sets (text-prompt CLIP at epoch 100 reaches the base-20-step level). OCR stays where every earlier run was: it moves between +0.000 and +0.026 across checkpoints, the final one is not significant in the in-training eval, and exact match does not move. New-seed confirmation (1024 + 1024 images): the alignment and quality gains hold (alignment CLIP and PickScore +0.0015*, text-prompt CLIP +0.007* and PickScore +0.0035*), the OCR gain does not (−0.007, n.s.). So the fixes made the reward reach the model for alignment, but OCR is still limited by the gradient noise of point 9.

How a result is checked

Something counts as an improvement only after an independent check. Three false gains taught this.

  1. Paired: model and base generate with the same seeds.
  2. New seeds, never used to choose checkpoints, and the checkpoint is chosen in advance.
  3. More images: 1024 per set (128 prompts × 8).
  4. Same GPU type: H200 and A100 give different images for the same seed.
  5. 95% bootstrap intervals; it counts only if the interval excludes zero.

Questions and next steps

  • NoisyCLIP on SD 1.5: retrain NoisyCLIP on SD 1.5 latents (with Vasco's code), or move to SDXL, where it works as published?
  • OCR: RL gets ~1–2 × 10⁻⁴ signal per image (point 9). Next: a differentiable text-recognition reward back-propagated through the last steps; fallback: a ~10× longer RL run with a 2-step random window and text-only prompts (~30–40 GPU-hours).
  • Which steps to train: only the steps where text forms (the "latent spark", ~steps 2–6) instead of all but the last?
  • Attribution: if the new run helps, which single change to isolate first?

Glossary

Each term in one sentence.

TermMeaning
GRPOGenerate a group of images per prompt; the model learns to do more like the best and less like the worst.
PRM / ORMProcess reward (scores mid-generation) / outcome reward (scores only the final image).
x̂₀The model's prediction, at each step, of the final image (as a latent).
z_tThe noisy latent at step t, i.e. what the model actually holds mid-generation.
VAE / TAESDDecoder from latent to image (expensive) / a small, fast imitation of it.
Φ (Phi)A fixed linear map from the latent's 4 channels to RGB (15 numbers), fitted for SD 1.5.
NoisyCLIPCLIP whose image side is fine-tuned to read noisy (SDXL) latents rendered as colour pictures.
LoRASmall trainable matrices on top of a frozen model.
CLIPScore / PickScoreImage–text match score / a second judge, not used in training.
SpearmanRank correlation: 1 = orders the images exactly like the final result.
Conformal Risk ControlPicks a threshold with a statistical guarantee that the risk is ≤ α.
KL penalty (β)Keeps the trained model close to the original; β sets how strongly.
Signal-to-noiseHow big the useful, consistent part of an update is compared with its random part.
