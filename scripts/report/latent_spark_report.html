Thesis progress · 6–10 November 2026

Steering the Latent Spark

Process-oriented alignment for Stable Diffusion 1.5: everything done since switching from SD 3.5 Medium to SD 1.5, how it was done, and what the numbers say.

At a glance
  • Phase 1 works: our own noise-aware reward model predicts the final image's alignment from the noisy latent, with no VAE decode, at 0.16 UNet steps per call (VAE + CLIP: 1.78). In the 10-step GRPO sampler it reaches Spearman 0.80 with the final score at step 5 and is the best early predictor of all methods tested.
  • Phase 2 works: conformal early pruning with that scorer saves ~22% of generation compute (groups of 8, prune once at step 6 of 10) with a calibrated 10% risk of losing quality. TAESD saves 10–18%, the VAE nothing.
  • Phase 3: no confirmed gain yet. GRPO on mixed alignment + text prompts with our process reward reached OCR 0.120 vs 0.094 (base, 10 steps) at its best checkpoint (epoch 60) on the in-training evaluation seeds (latest checkpoint: 0.119, not significant), but re-evaluated on new seeds (1024 + 1024 images) the gain shrinks to +0.005 (95% CI −0.008…+0.017, not significant). Alignment is unchanged; the CLIP-outcome-reward baseline is also flat on OCR and slightly worse on PickScore. The policy now moves (approx_kl ~2e-4), but 60 epochs are not enough to close the gap to 20 steps (OCR +0.049).
  • Supervisor's curricula: the weight curriculum (alignment first, OCR ramped in) gives the most consistent text gain so far in the in-training evaluation (OCR +0.005 to +0.022 at every checkpoint, significant at epochs 30, 40 and 90) with alignment unchanged; the steps curriculum (4 → 10 steps) finished, its checkpoints are being evaluated; the run with both is training. Confirmation on new seeds is running.
  • The earlier OCR-only GRPO runs did not work (8 runs, every independent check within noise). Diagnosis: SD 1.5 rarely renders text and the policy barely moved (approx_kl ~1e-7). The Phase 3 setup moves it ~1000× more.
  • Step reduction target: base SD 1.5 at 10 steps keeps 61% of its 50-step OCR. Alignment (CLIP) saturates at 10–20 steps; text keeps improving to 50.

1. Switch from SD 3.5 Medium to SD 1.5

The starting point was this repo's Flow-GRPO fork training SD 3.5 Medium on one GPU with an OCR reward (scripts/train_sd3_fast.py). SD 1.5 is an epsilon-prediction diffusion model, not a flow-matching one, so the GRPO sampler had to be rebuilt:

  • Stochastic DDIM step with an exact Gaussian log-probability (flow_grpo/diffusers_patch/ddim_with_logprob.py), DDPO-style, used inside an "SDE window" of steps; deterministic DDIM elsewhere (sd15_pipeline_with_logprob.py).
  • Trainer scripts/train_sd15.py (LoRA rank 32 on the UNet attention, GRPO loss with PPO clipping and a KL term), configs general_ocr_sd15_* in config/grpo.py. 10 sampling steps for training.
  • The plan's “t ∈ [1.0, 0.5]” maps to the high-noise half of the DDIM schedule: steps 1–5 of 10 (timesteps 901 → 501).

Diagnostics showed the LoRA was updating but the policy barely moved.

What we learned from it

  • The reward signal existed, the policy did not move. No zero-variance groups, within-group reward std ≈ 22, but approx_kl ≈ 1e-7 per update. With clip range 1e-4, 86% of the samples in every second update were clipped away. CLIPScore rose slightly (+0.002 to +0.009): training changed the model, not towards legible text.
  • SD 1.5 rarely renders the target text (exact match ~2%), so most groups contain no good example to reinforce.
  • Evaluation pitfalls found on the way: the default 40-step eval did not measure the 10-step sampler being trained; a fully stochastic eval sampler produced a spurious +0.036 gain; and in-training evals (512 images, many looks) produced two false “gains” (z = 2.5 and z = 1.8) that vanished on independent checks. Since then every claim needs an independent paired check (section 8).

5. Phase 1 · A noise-aware process reward model

Goal of the phase: score text–image alignment of the noisy latent zt without a VAE decode. The NoisyCLIP code in the repo (flow_grpo/noisyclip_scorer.py) turned out to be a randomly initialised, untrained adapter for 16-channel SD 3 latents, and the published NoisyCLIP weights were not available, so we built and trained our own.

5.1 Untrained NoisyCLIP Option A (linear projection)

x̂₀ → affine 4→3 projection Φ to a 64×64 RGB image → CLIP ViT-L/14. Φ fitted by least squares on 32 held-out prompts (R² 0.82–0.86). Cost 0.10 UNet steps. It detects a random wrong prompt early (93% at step 6 of 50, 100% from step 14), but its correlation with the final CLIP score plateaus at 0.65 even at the last step: CLIP on a 64×64 linear projection is not the same signal as CLIP on the decoded image. Projecting the raw zt instead of x̂₀ is useless before ~55% of the trajectory.

5.2 Our trained scorer

  • Data (scripts/phase1_make_data.py): 1440 GenAI-Bench training prompts × 2 seeds, 50-step DDIM; Φ(x̂₀), x̂₀ at 25 steps and the CLIP embedding of the final image. The 32 calibration + 128 evaluation prompts are excluded.
  • Model (scripts/phase1_train_scorer.py): CLIP ViT-L/14 vision tower + LoRA r16 + a timestep embedding (3.4 M trainable parameters), distilled to predict the final image's CLIP embedding from any step. Score = cosine with the prompt's CLIP text embedding, i.e. a predicted final CLIPScore. 8 minutes of training on one H200.
  • v2 (latent input): a small conv decoder on the 4-channel x̂₀ adds a learned residual at 256×256 on top of Φ. Raises the late plateau (Spearman 0.85 → 0.90 at the end, 0.78 → 0.82 at 40%).
  • Final version logs/phase1_scorer/v2_lat_10steps_eta1: v2 retrained on 10-step stochastic trajectories (1440 prompts × 4 seeds), the setting GRPO uses.

6. Phase 2 · Calibrated early pruning

scripts/phase2_conformal_pruning.py. In a group of G trajectories for one prompt, at a checkpoint step the scorer scores every live trajectory; those far below the group leader stop. The threshold λ is calibrated with Conformal Risk Control on half of the 128 held-out prompts and tested on the other half (40–200 random splits). Scores are put on one scale per step by the within-group spread (a simple version of the slides' drift correction). Data: 10 steps, stochastic sampling, final scorer.

  • Measured test risk is 0.09–0.10 in every row: the conformal guarantee holds. At α = 0.2 the saving is 23–24%.
  • tprune ≈ step 6 of 10 (60% of the trajectory). Several pruning points (3+5, 4+6, 2+4+6) with one shared threshold do not beat a single one at step 6.
  • The ceiling is structural: a trajectory pruned at step 6 only saves the remaining 40% of its steps, and before step 4–5 the scorer is not reliable enough to prune harder. The typical spread within a group is 0.021 CLIP; the image lost when the “best” is pruned is usually within 0.002–0.003 of the kept one.

Supervisor's curricula

Two schedulers suggested by the supervisor, each tested on top of the PRM run (one change at a time, plus both):

  • Reward-weight curriculum: alignment first, text later. Over the first 60 epochs the OCR weight rises 0 → 80 and the alignment weight falls 100 → 50 (it never reaches zero, so alignment is not forgotten). Because advantages are normalised within each prompt's group, only the ratio matters, and only on prompts with a text target.
  • Denoising-steps curriculum: 4 → 6 → 8 → 10 steps, 20 epochs each. The suggestion was 2 → 10, but SD 1.5 with 2 steps produces images with no usable content (with 4 steps base OCR is already only 0.047), so groups would carry no signal. The first epochs are 2–2.5× cheaper to sample. The PRM is queried at 40/60/80/100% of whatever trajectory length is current, and gradient accumulation is done manually so every epoch still makes 2 optimizer steps.

Does the PRM still work with fewer steps? It was trained only on the 10-step grid, so we re-checked it (128 held-out prompts × 4, eta = 1). Spearman with the final CLIP score / within-prompt: 4 steps: step 2 0.67 / 0.35, step 3 0.83 / 0.63; 6 steps: step 3 0.71 / 0.44, step 4 0.81 / 0.58, step 5 0.86 / 0.64. That is about as good as at 10 steps at the same fraction of the trajectory, so the steps curriculum gets a usable reward. At the last step the VAE is better (0.98 vs 0.83 at 4 steps), as at 10 steps.

Details of every Phase 3 run

All runs share: SD 1.5 + LoRA (rank 32), mixed prompt set (1440 GenAI-Bench + 1088 text prompts, held-out evaluation prompts excluded), 32 prompts × 8 images per epoch, stochastic DDIM (eta = 1) at every step, every transition trained, per-prompt advantage normalisation, PPO clip 1e-3, KL penalty β = 0.04, learning rate 3e-4, 2 optimizer steps per epoch, 8-bit Adam, one H200, checkpoint every 10 epochs, each checkpoint evaluated at 10 deterministic steps on the same held-out prompts and seeds. 8. How results are checked

  • Paired evaluation: every model generates from the same prompts and the same initial noise, so a difference comes from the model alone. Differences are averaged per prompt with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval.
  • Same GPU type: the A100 and H200 produce different images for the same seed (base OCR 0.113 vs 0.131), so compared runs always share a GPU type.
  • Independent check before claiming a gain: in-training evals produced two false positives. A gain counts only after a separate evaluation on new seeds with more images (1024 per set).
  • Held-out prompts everywhere: the 160 GenAI-Bench calibration/evaluation prompts and the 128 OCR test words never appear in any training data.
  • Cost in UNet-step units: scorer time per image divided by one UNet step (with CFG) on the same GPU.

9. Questions for the meeting

  1. Task: alignment on GenAI-Bench plus text rendering (current choice), or one of the two?
  2. Teacher model: CLIP limits the PRM on counting (76%) and relations (66%). Move to a VQA-based teacher (VQAScore, GenAI-Bench's own metric) or a VLM judge?
  3. NoisyCLIP: can we get Vasco's code and weights to compare with our scorer?
  4. Reward shape (slides): we use a smooth margin (cosine). Should step rewards become strict thresholds?
  5. KL penalty: β = 0.04 so far. How to tune it against reward hacking without collapsing diversity?
  6. Where to steer (slides): our data says the layout and text form at steps 3–5 of 10 and the PRM becomes reliable at step 4–5. Restrict Phase 3 updates to that window?
